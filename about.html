Research Philosophy & Vision

Modern medicine is confronted by staggering complexity: human disease is governed by multi-scale networks ranging from genomic sequences and chromatin conformations to cellular metabolisms and clinical manifestations.

My research operates at this frontier. I develop novel machine learning paradigms—predominantly **geometric deep learning, graph transformers, and causal representation learning**—tailored to the non-Euclidean topologies inherent in biological systems. Rather than viewing machine learning as a pure black box, my laboratory builds mathematically grounded models that researchers and clinicians can audit, inspect, and trust.

Prior to my postdoctoral fellowship at Stanford, I completed my Ph.D. in Computer Science with a focus on topological representations for macromolecular structures. Over the past six years, my work has contributed to pioneering algorithms in drug-target interaction screening and multi-modal clinical prognosis.

Geometric Deep Learning

Formulating invariant and equivariant graph representations for non-Euclidean manifolds like proteins, small molecules, and neural connectomes.

Trustworthy AI & Safety

Developing uncertainty calibration, mechanistic interpretability, and robust clinical benchmarks to prevent algorithmic bias and hallucination.
